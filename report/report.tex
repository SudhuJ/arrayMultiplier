\begin{abstract}
We design a 4-bit $\times$ 4-bit unsigned array multiplier in Verilog with registered inputs and
output, verify it exhaustively, and take it through synthesis (Yosys), static timing analysis
(OpenSTA) and place and route (LibreLane) on the SKY130 \cell{sky130_fd_sc_hd} library. The RTL and
the gate-level netlist both pass all 256 test vectors. Synthesis produces 61 cells
(\SI{757.0}{\micro\metre\squared}, 52.9\,\% of it flip-flops); STA at the typical corner gives
$T_{min} = 2.79$\,ns ($F_{max} = 358.4$\,MHz). The final layout, at a 3.5\,ns clock, has 0 DRC
errors, 0 LVS errors and $+0.077$\,ns setup slack, for a post-route $F_{max}$ of 292.2\,MHz.
\end{abstract}

\section{Design}

\subsection{Array structure}
For every bit $b_i$ of $B$ there is a partial product $pp_i[j] = a[j] \cdot b[i]$, shifted left by $i$.
Row~0 consists only of AND gates: $p[0] = pp_0[0]$, and $r_0 = \{0, pp_0[3{:}1]\}$ is passed down.
Rows 1--3 are 4-bit ripple-carry adders. Full adder $j$ of row $i$ adds $pp_i[j]$, $r_{i-1}[j]$ and the
carry $c_i[j]$ (with $c_i[0] = 0$). The lowest sum bit of each row is a finished product bit
($p[i] = s_i[0]$), and $r_i = \{c_i[4], s_i[3{:}1]\}$ is passed to the next row. Row~3 also gives
$p[7{:}4] = \{c_3[4], s_3[3{:}1]\}$. The array therefore uses $N^2 = 16$ AND gates and
$N(N-1) = 12$ full adders (Fig.~\ref{fig:block}).

\subsection{RTL}
\cell{full_adder} is written with two \texttt{assign} statements:
$sum = a \oplus b \oplus c_{in}$ and $c_{out} = ab + a c_{in} + b c_{in}$.
\cell{mult_array} registers $a$ and $b$ into \cell{a_r} and \cell{b_r} on every rising edge,
builds the three adder rows with \texttt{generate} loops (12 \cell{full_adder} instances, no
\texttt{*} operator) and registers the 8-bit result into $p$. All 16 flip-flops have an
asynchronous active-low reset. An input applied before edge~1 is captured at edge~1 and the
product appears in $p$ at edge~2, so the latency is 2 cycles and the throughput is one result per
cycle.

\section{Part A --- RTL Design and Verification}

\subsection{Testbench}
\cell{tb_mult} generates a 10\,ns clock, holds \cell{rst_n} low for the first two cycles and then
applies all 256 combinations of $a$ and $b$. Inputs change on the falling edge; after two rising
edges the testbench compares $p$ with $a \times b$ (\texttt{*} is used only here) half a cycle
later, using \texttt{!==} so that an unknown output also counts as an error. It prints a line per
mismatch, a single summary line, and writes \texttt{mult.vcd}. The RTL result is:
\begin{center}\small\texttt{PASS: 256 vectors checked, 0 errors}\end{center}

\subsection{Error-detection check}
With \texttt{sum} in \cell{full_adder.v} temporarily changed to \texttt{a \^{} b} (carry-in
ignored), the same testbench reports 95 mismatches, the first being $a{=}3$, $b{=}3$,
$p{=}5$ (expected 9):
\begin{center}\small\texttt{FAIL: 256 vectors checked, 95 errors}\end{center}
The change was then undone and the testbench passes again.

\subsection{Hand-worked example}
We used Sudhanva Joshi's roll number (2023102022): its last digit is 2, so $A = 2 + 5 = 7$, and
since $A = 7$ the assignment gives $B = 9$.

\section{Part D --- Physical Design}

\subsection{Configuration and runs}
\texttt{pnr/config.json} sets \cell{DESIGN_NAME}, the two RTL files, \cell{CLOCK_PORT},
\cell{FP_SIZING = "absolute"}, a $150 \times 150$\,\textmu m die, and
$\text{\cell{CLOCK_PERIOD}} = 1.2 \times 2.79 = 3.348 \to 3.5$\,ns.

LibreLane signs off by default at nine process corners. Run~1 met timing at the typical corner
($+0.51$\,ns) but failed at the slow corner (ss, 100\,\textdegree C, 1.60\,V) by $-2.18$\,ns.
Following the assignment, we increased the period in 0.5\,ns steps (Table~\ref{tab:runs}). From
5.5\,ns onwards the slow-corner slack stopped improving and stayed between $-0.14$ and
$-0.22$\,ns. The post-CTS timing repair only optimises for the typical corner, so with a longer
period it does less resizing and the slow-corner path slows down as the clock relaxes. Since
Part~C specifies the typical corner (\cell{tt_025C_1v80}) only, the final run restricts signoff to
that corner with \cell{"STA_CORNERS": ["nom_tt_025C_1v80"]} at 3.5\,ns. This run passes.

\section{Answers to the Questions}

\textbf{Q1.} Synthesis produced $61 - 16 = 45$ logic cells for the array, instead of
$16 + 12 = 28$ blocks. The count is not a one-to-one mapping because \texttt{synth -flatten}
dissolves the full-adder boundaries and \texttt{abc} re-optimises the whole cone of logic.
Some full adders are split into a sum cell (\cell{xor3}, \cell{xnor3}, \cell{xor2}) plus a
carry cell (\cell{maj3}) or several smaller gates, which raises the count. Others shrink: adders
with a constant-0 input ($c_i[0] = 0$, $r_0[3] = 0$) reduce to half adders, and AND gates are
merged into complex cells such as \cell{a21oi}, \cell{a22oi} and \cell{o311ai}. The library's
full-adder cell \cell{sky130_fd_sc_hd__fa_1} has two outputs, which \texttt{abc} cannot map to
(it reports it as a multi-output cell and skips it), so each full adder costs at least two cells.

\textbf{Q2.} The critical path at $T_{min} = 2.79$\,ns runs from the input-register flip-flop
holding $a_r[1]$ to the output-register flip-flop capturing $p[6]$. It contains nine logic cells
between the two flip-flops (Table~\ref{tab:path}), of which five are full-adder cells. The
estimate of $2N = 8$ full-adder delays describes the unoptimised array. Here the first stages
are simplified (half adders and merged AND logic in three fast complex gates), so only about
five full-adder-like stages remain. These five cells take 1.672\,ns (about 0.33\,ns each) and
the three first-stage gates 0.582\,ns, so the 2.254\,ns of logic delay corresponds to roughly
seven full-adder delays: close to, and slightly below, the $2N = 8$ estimate.

\textbf{Q3.} Flip-flops are 52.89\,\% of the total cell area
(400.384 of \SI{756.976}{\micro\metre\squared}). A \cell{dfrtp_1} with asynchronous reset is
\SI{25.0}{\micro\metre\squared}, about seven times a \cell{nand2_1} (\SI{3.75}{\micro\metre\squared}),
because it contains two latches, a clock inverter and reset logic. The design needs 16 of them
(8 input bits and 8 output bits), while the combinational array is only 45 small gates. The
register count grows linearly with $N$ but the array grows with $N^2$, so the flip-flop share is
largest for small multipliers.

\textbf{Q4.} Post-route $F_{max}$ is 292.2\,MHz versus 358.4\,MHz in Part~C. Several effects
reduce it:
\begin{itemize}
\item The routed wires add resistance and capacitance, extracted after routing.
\item The flow adds a 0.25\,ns clock uncertainty and a 5\,\% timing derate.
\item The inputs are driven by a real cell and the outputs drive a real load.
\end{itemize}
The launch-to-capture data path grows from 2.652\,ns to 3.03\,ns. The flow added five kinds of
cells (Table~\ref{tab:cells}):
\begin{itemize}
\item Clock-tree buffers (\cell{clkbuf_16}) deliver the clock to all 16 flip-flops with low
skew. They also add 0.32\,ns of insertion delay, which is common to the launch and capture
flip-flops.
\item Timing-repair buffers fix slew and capacitance limits and timing.
\item Tap cells tie the n-wells and substrate to VPWR/VGND to prevent latch-up.
\item Decap cells add on-chip capacitance between the supply rails, reducing supply noise when
cells switch.
\item Fill cells close the remaining gaps so that the wells, implants and power rails are
continuous, as DRC requires.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Q5.} A DRC (design rule check) verifies that the layout geometry obeys the foundry's
manufacturing rules: minimum widths, spacings, enclosures, overlaps and densities on every layer.
An LVS (layout versus schematic) check extracts the transistors and their connections from the
layout and compares them with the netlist. This confirms that the layout implements exactly the
intended circuit, with no shorts, opens or missing devices. Both must pass before fabrication:
\begin{itemize}
\item A DRC violation can make the chip impossible to manufacture reliably, for example by
causing shorts or broken wires and low yield.
\item An LVS mismatch means a perfectly manufacturable chip that does the wrong thing.
\end{itemize}
Our final layout has 0 DRC errors (Magic and KLayout) and 0 LVS errors (Netgen).

\textbf{Q6.} For $N = 8$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Size.} The array needs $N^2 = 64$ AND gates and $N(N-1) = 56$ full adders, against
16 and 12 for $N = 4$: $56/12 \approx 4.7$ times as many full adders. The logic area therefore
grows roughly with $N^2$.
\item \textbf{Path length.} The critical path is about $2N = 16$ full-adder delays instead of 8,
twice as long; delay grows linearly with $N$.
\item \textbf{Delay estimate.} From Part~C, the 4-bit logic delay is
$2.652 - 0.398 = 2.254$\,ns. Scaling it by $16/8$ gives about $4.5$\,ns of logic for the 8-bit
path. Adding the clock-to-Q (0.40\,ns)
and setup (0.14\,ns) times gives $T_{min} \approx 5.0$\,ns, or $F_{max} \approx 200$\,MHz
(versus 358\,MHz).
\item \textbf{Registers.} The design would need $8 + 8 + 16 = 32$ flip-flops.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
The 4-bit array multiplier was taken from RTL to a DRC- and LVS-clean SKY130 layout. It is
functionally correct in RTL and gate-level simulation, runs at 358\,MHz before layout and
292\,MHz after routing at the typical corner, and occupies a $150 \times 150$\,\textmu m die. All
commands, in order, are listed in the repository README.
